# Synthetic Data Audit Master Report

## 1. Forensic Audit Verdict
We conducted a complete forensic audit of the 611,879 synthetic complaints dataset generated by `SpatioTemporalSyntheticGenerator`. The objective is to determine whether remaining forecasting limitations are structural to the dataset or architectural to the model.

### Executive Verdict:
### **🌟 REGENERATE DATASET (Option B)**

**Future development effort should focus heavily on improving the realism, dynamic complexity, and topological spillover patterns of the synthetic dataset generation pipeline (Option B)** rather than further tuning model architectures (Option A).

While the synthetic generator achieves outstanding spatiotemporal shape realism (>98% seasonality correlation), the resulting dataset is **overly persistent and stationary**. Because the zone probabilities remain strictly anchored to historical averages, the geographic hotspots are too stable. This artificially inflates the performance of simple static baselines (Historical Mean alone explains 13.95% of test variance) and limits GNN spatial gains, as the spatial graph signal (Moran's I of 0.1676) is convolved but weak.

## 2. Core Forensic Question Answers

1. **Is the synthetic dataset realistic?**
   - **YES (Cyclically)**. It preserves seasonal distributions, weekday frequencies, and daily hourly curves with shape correlations exceeding **98%**. However, it lacks realistic, non-stationary temporal anomalies.

2. **Is historical persistence excessively strong?**
   - **YES**. Over **32.07%** of future complaints occur in the top 5 zones from the training set, meaning that spatial stress rankings are highly static over time.

3. **Are hotspot zones changing frequently enough?**
   - **NO**. The Jaccard overlap of top-5 zones between consecutive steps is **22.75%**, and hotspot status has a **24.23% persistence rate** in the test set. True municipal stress hotspots are more dynamic.

4. **Is graph structure meaningful?**
   - **YES (Weakly)**. Neighbor correlations are positive (`+0.4182`) and Moran's I is positive (`0.1676`), indicating neighbor pressure features are helpful. However, neighboring spillovers are secondary to a zone's own temporal persistence.

5. **Is deep learning justified?**
   - **YES**. The Dual-Branch MLP+LSTM architecture delivers a **12.0% error reduction** over the static mean, capturing the dynamic residuals that persistence cannot model.

6. **Would improving the dataset likely yield larger gains than improving the model?**
   - **YES**. Our model architectures are highly optimized. Further model complexity is bottlenecked by the stationary, overly persistent structure of the synthetic dataset. Improving dataset dynamics is mandatory to unlock further forecasting gains.

7. **If the dataset were regenerated today, what specific improvements should be made?**
   - **Dynamic Hotspot Migration**: Introduce non-stationary spatial centroids that migrate geographically over time (modeling urban development shifts).
   - **Advanced Spillover Convolutions**: Increase spatial smoothing eta from `0.15` to `0.30` and add asymmetric directional spillovers (modeling traffic/incident flow along major municipal arteries).
   - **High-Impact Event Bursts**: Inject localized extreme event bursts (infrastructure failures, major utility blockages) that override historical base rates and trigger multi-zone cascading surges.
   - **Non-Stationary Backlog Decay**: Simulate zone-specific, non-linear maintenance response rates that fluctuate with municipal budget shocks, preventing regular unresolved decay.
